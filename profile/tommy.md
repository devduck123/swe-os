These are my preferences and tendencies, not universal rules. The project, the problem, and anything I say directly take priority.

## How I think

- I'm casual, but not unserious. Something doesn't need to sound formal for me to take it seriously.
- I'm skeptical by default. My first reaction is often some version of "does that actually make sense?", followed by pushing the reasoning one layer deeper.
- I want reasoning I can interrogate, not authority. "Best practice" doesn't convince me by itself. Tell me what problem it solves or what failure it prevents.
- I like strong takes. I'll challenge one quickly if the assumptions smell wrong, and I'll change my mind just as quickly when the evidence changes.
- I often understand an idea by pushing against it a little. Disagreement is part of how I refine the model.
- I'd rather get a clear recommendation than six technically valid options with no judgment behind them.
- I care more about understanding why something works than about sounding like I know the right terms.

## What I care about

- **Taste.** Clean architecture, clean UI, good abstractions, good writing, good products. A lot of things work. Fewer are actually good.
- **Simplicity I can explain.** If I can't say why a component, dependency, or abstraction exists, that's usually a smell.
- **Complete features.** The happy path is a demo. A real feature handles its loading, empty, error, and edge states, and comes with proof that it works.
- **UI as engineering.** Accessibility, responsiveness, and usability deserve the same seriousness as backend design.
- **Speed with judgment.** I like building fast, especially with AI. Speed isn't an excuse for creating garbage I'll have to untangle later.
- **Proportion.** A side project doesn't need Google's infrastructure. A production system shouldn't inherit side-project shortcuts by accident.
- **Reversible decisions.** I move quickly when a choice is cheap to undo, and slow down when it isn't.

I'm ambitious about getting better. I'm not interested in turning software engineering into a grindset personality.

## How I like agents to work with me

- **Be autonomous.** Keep moving on work that's reversible and inside the boundaries I've already set.
- **Keep up.** Build on what I already understand instead of restarting from the beginner explanation.
- **Show receipts.** Tell me what you checked, ran, or observed when the answer depends on evidence.
- **Push back.** If I'm optimizing the wrong thing, overengineering, or leaning on a weak assumption, tell me.
- **Don't make decisions opaque.** Use AI aggressively for the mechanical work. I don't need to perform every step myself, but I should be able to understand and reconstruct the important ones.

The exact operating rules, like when to stop and ask, live in [Using SWE OS with an agent](../skills/README.md#rules-for-every-task).

## What I avoid

- Architecture for a company we don't have.
- Complexity whose payoff nobody can explain.
- Rules that outlive the problem that created them.
- Engineering ceremony without an engineering reason.
